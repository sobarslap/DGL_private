# PART 1 — VOICEOVER SCRIPT (clean read)

**Direction:** calm, matter-of-fact, a colleague giving practical advice. Never salesy.
Three gear changes — Acts 1–2 intimate and slow, Act 3 flat and hard (reading evidence
aloud), Acts 4–7 brisk and practical. Beat of silence before "Not chasing — searching,"
before "A chase with facts is a deadline," and before "That's what Dogetlawyer is for."

> "We'll pay Friday." It's the fourth Friday.
>
> You've rewritten the same message three times. And still haven't sent it.
>
> The work is delivered. The invoice is overdue. And your bills haven't moved — rent, payroll, supplier. All due Friday.
>
> The worst part isn't the money. It's not knowing where you stand. What did we agree? When were they meant to pay? Can you prove you delivered?
>
> If that's you — you're not bad at business. You're in the majority.
>
> At any one time, twenty-six billion pounds is sitting in overdue invoices across the UK.
>
> Thirty-eight businesses close every day because of late payment.
>
> And the firms affected lose eighty-six hours a year chasing it — more than two working weeks.
>
> Here's what those hours really are. Not chasing — searching. The agreement's in an email thread. The signed copy's on someone's laptop. The scope was agreed in a message nobody saved.
>
> So the chase goes out soft. Because a chase without facts is asking for a favour. A chase with facts is a deadline.
>
> Every agreement you sign needs four things you can find in thirty seconds. The agreed payment date. What you agreed to deliver. Proof you delivered it. And who owns the next step.
>
> That's what Dogetlawyer is for. Every agreement in one register — not an inbox, not a drive, not someone's memory.
>
> Open a contract and the answer is already there. Who owns it. The date that was agreed. The next task, with a name against it. Plain English, built for UK small businesses — no legal team required.
>
> So the next chase writes itself. The agreed date. The completed work. The invoice.
>
> Send this to whoever's chasing invoices.

**Word count:** ~300 · **Target pace:** ~150 wpm
