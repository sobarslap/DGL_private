## Voiceover, clean read (~265 words)

> Delivery: fast and punchy for the hook (0:00–0:14), then a "myth / truth" rhythm, a beat of silence on each strike-through. Friendly, a bit cheeky, never preachy.

This one line just cost a firm fifty-four grand.
Their machine broke. They lost fifty-eight thousand. The supplier said: we'll pay three nine.
And a line just like it is probably in your supplier terms right now.
So let's bust four myths that leave businesses out of pocket.

Myth one. It's in the contract, so I'm stuck with it.
Not quite. If it's on their standard terms, the cap has to be reasonable. And it's on them to prove it. Not you.

Myth two. It's judged on the day it all went wrong.
Nope. It's judged on what you both knew when you signed. So what was said back then really matters.

Myth three. Every cap is a rip-off.
Plenty are perfectly fair, and they hold up. Courts weigh things like who had the clout, and whether you knew the clause was there.

Myth four. Keeping records is just admin.
It's your evidence. One firm had a phone call and a lost quote. The other had the signed terms, the emails and eleven past orders. Guess who had a case to make.

Same clause. Same supplier. The difference was the paper trail.

That's what Dogetlawyer's for. Every supplier, and the cap buried in their terms, pulled out where you can see it.
Open one up: the version you signed, when it was sent, the clause flagged, and every order on those terms.

Here's your sixty-second challenge. Open your biggest supplier contract and search for the word liability. You might be surprised.
Which myth did you believe? Drop the number in the comments.
And send this to whoever signs your supplier terms.
