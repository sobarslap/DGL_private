> Delivery: chatty, slightly alarmed on the viewer's behalf, then calm and practical through the checklist. A beat after "Personally."

You run a limited company. So your house is safe, right?
So why could a supplier come after your house?
It usually starts like this. A friendly message, a credit account form. Just sign at the bottom. And on page four, there's a box.
It's called a personal guarantee. If your company can't pay, you're on the hook. Personally.
That limited company protection you were counting on? For that debt, it's gone. Your savings, your car, even your home could be at risk.
And here's the kicker. A guarantee only has to be in writing and signed. Which is exactly what that little form is.

So before you sign anything, check these five things.
One. Search the whole thing for guarantee, and indemnity. Every page. The small print too.
Two. Is there a limit? An unlimited guarantee covers every penny the company owes them. You can ask for a cap.
Three. How do you get out of it? Can you end it with notice? What happens if you leave the business?
Four. Who else is signing? If it's joint and several, each of you could be chased for the full amount.
And five. If your home's on the line, get proper advice before you sign. Not after.

The worst part? It's easy to forget you ever signed one. Until the letter turns up.
That's where Dogetlawyer comes in. Every contract in one place, and every guarantee on record, with who signed it and whether there's a limit.
So you always know what you've put on the line.
Save this for the next time someone says, just a quick signature. And send it to whoever signs for your business.
